MODULE 01

Heikin Ashi

Heikin Ashi uses averaged candle calculations, so the candles look smoother than standard candlesticks. The visual goal is to make the prevailing trend easier to follow.

Read the candle: Strong bodies with small opposing shadows generally show persistent directional pressure. Smaller bodies and larger shadows suggest hesitation or a transition may be developing.

Trend Up

Repeated bullish Heikin Ashi candles with strong bodies can visually highlight an uptrend. Always check the broader structure before acting.

Trend Down

Repeated bearish Heikin Ashi candles can visually highlight a downtrend. A change in candle character is not, by itself, a guaranteed reversal.

Candlestick Language

Start with what the candle actually tells you: open, high, low and close.

Bullish Candle

Close is above the open. A large body means the period moved decisively in one direction.

Bearish Candle

Close is below the open. A large body indicates strong selling pressure during that period.

Doji

Open and close are close together. It often represents indecision; context determines whether it matters.

Hammer

Small body near the top with a long lower shadow. After a decline, it can show rejection of lower prices. Confirmation and location matter.

Shooting Star

Small body near the lower part with a long upper shadow. After an advance, it can show rejection of higher prices.

Marubozu

A candle with a very large body and little or no wick. It shows strong directional movement for that period.

Spinning Top

Small body with relatively prominent shadows. It signals uncertainty rather than a direction by itself.

More Patterns

Bullish Engulfing

A larger bullish candle fully engulfs the previous bearish body. Stronger when it forms at a meaningful support area after a decline.

Bearish Engulfing

A larger bearish candle fully engulfs the previous bullish body. Stronger when it forms near resistance after an advance.

Morning Star

A three-candle bullish reversal structure: bearish candle, small indecision candle, then a strong bullish candle.

Evening Star

The bearish counterpart: bullish candle, small indecision candle, then a strong bearish candle.

Piercing Line

A bullish two-candle formation in which the second candle closes deeply into the prior bearish body.

Dark Cloud Cover

A bearish two-candle formation in which the second candle closes deeply into the prior bullish body.

Three White Soldiers

Three consecutive strong bullish candles, often interpreted as sustained buying pressure.

Three Black Crows

Three consecutive strong bearish candles, often interpreted as sustained selling pressure.

Chart Patterns

Chart patterns describe multi-candle price structures. They are not automatic trade signals.

REVERSAL

Double Top

Price forms two prominent highs around a similar area, with a valley between them. A neckline break is commonly watched for confirmation.

REVERSAL

Double Bottom

Price forms two notable lows around a similar area, with a peak between them. Traders often watch the intervening peak as a confirmation level.

REVERSAL

Head & Shoulders

Three peaks with the middle peak higher than the two outer peaks. The neckline is the key structural level.

REVERSAL

Inverse Head & Shoulders

Three troughs with the middle trough lower than the two outer troughs. The neckline is watched for confirmation.

CONTINUATION

Flag

A short consolidation after a strong directional move. The breakout direction is often monitored as a continuation signal.

CONTINUATION / BREAKOUT

Triangle

Price compresses between converging boundaries. Ascending, descending and symmetrical forms are common.

REVERSAL

Wedges

Price compresses inside converging trendlines. Rising and falling wedges can appear before continuation or reversal moves.

RANGE

Rectangle

Price oscillates between horizontal support and resistance. A confirmed breakout can signal expansion from the range.
